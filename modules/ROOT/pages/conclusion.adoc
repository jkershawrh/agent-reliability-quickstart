= Review and Next Steps
:navtitle: Conclusion

== You qualified a reliability pattern

You began with a NOC alarm and ended with evidence that the agent behaves
differently—and explicitly—under four conditions:

* a healthy request returns a sourced recommendation that still requires human approval;
* prompt injection produces abstention before tools or inference;
* an unauthorized action is denied by application policy and remains blocked by OpenShift identity; and
* inference disruption produces a degraded response instead of a fabricated diagnosis.

You then ran those expectations as a Pipeline, connected behavioral proof to
GitOps recovery ownership, and exported a reusable reliability review.

== The pattern you can reuse

The reusable architecture is not tied to telecommunications. Another domain
can replace the alarm, telemetry, and runbook tools while retaining the same
control contract:

. screen untrusted input before reasoning;
. expose only named, least-privilege tools;
. preserve independent platform authorization;
. require evidence before a recommendation;
. bound remote inference with timeouts and retries;
. return explicit abstained, denied, and degraded states;
. attach traceable decision metadata; and
. keep consequential action behind accountable human approval.

The exported `AgentReliabilityProfile` captures the configurable portion of
that contract. The qualification scorecard proves the deployed behavior at one
revision. Both are needed: configuration without evidence is an intention, and
evidence without versioned expectations is difficult to reproduce.

== What remains before production

This lab does not prove that a model can diagnose a live network safely. A
production program still needs representative incident evaluation, adversarial
testing beyond one injected phrase, identity and network-policy review, durable
tracing, privacy and retention controls, capacity testing, model and prompt
change management, rollback exercises, human-factors testing, and named owners
for incident response and approval.

Reliability is also temporal. A passing release can regress when a model,
prompt, tool, policy, image, or platform component changes. Treat the Pipeline
as a release gate and recurring check, not a one-time certificate.

== Questions for the design review

Bring five questions to the next architecture review:

. Which decisions must remain deterministic even when model quality improves?
. Which evidence sources can be trusted, and how is their provenance verified?
. What is the maximum authority available to each service identity and tool?
. Which unavailable or uncertain states must cause abstention or degradation?
. What release evidence is required after a model, prompt, tool, policy, or
  platform change?

These questions keep the discussion centered on observable controls instead of
model fluency. They also make ownership visible: application teams own policy
and response contracts, platform teams own isolation and identity, AI teams own
model and guardrail qualification, domain operators own approval criteria, and
security and governance teams define acceptable risk and audit evidence.

== Recommended next steps

Review the generated profile assessment with platform, security, AI, and domain
owners. Replace simulated tools only after defining their authorization and
evidence contracts. Add domain-specific failure cases before increasing tool
privilege. Finally, qualify the intended Launchpad seat count and preserve the
image digests, source revision, PipelineRun, and scorecard as release evidence.

The durable lesson is simple: a useful agent recommends; a reliable system
proves why that recommendation was allowed, what it used, and what happens when
any dependency fails.
